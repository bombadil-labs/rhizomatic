# NOTE — Relational Completeness: Rhizomatic vs. Codd's Algebra

**Status:** Note (informative record of a settled result; the normative pins live in SPEC-2 §6/§10,
ERRATA-2 E21, and `vectors/l1-eval/eval-relational.json`)
**Concerns:** SPEC-1, SPEC-2, SPEC-5, SPEC-7
**Settles:** SPEC-2 §6's flagged open edge ("confirm or refute" ad-hoc product) and §10's
"ad-hoc join" open question. (2026-07-30, issue #31)

---

## 0. The result in three sentences

The **selective fragment** of Codd's algebra — selection against constants with full boolean
closure, union, difference, intersection, and projection-with-lineage — is expressed exactly by
short L2 terms over a natural encoding of relations as delta sets (Theorem 1). **Cartesian
product is inexpressible in pure L2 — provably** (Theorem 2), because no operator in the algebra
can mint a delta (Lemma 1), and that same lemma *is* the §5 complexity envelope and the §1
federation sandbox, so the gap is load-bearing rather than accidental. The **system** — L2 terms
composed with L7 derivations whose outputs re-enter as signed deltas — is relationally complete
by stratification (Theorem 3), so Rhizomatic is *fully relational*, with the one operation that
fabricates tuples relocated to the layer where fabrication carries an identity.

## 1. What must be proven

Codd's relational algebra (1970; completeness formalized 1972) is generated by six primitives
over relations-as-sets-of-tuples: selection σ_θ, projection π_A, Cartesian product ×, union ∪,
set difference −, and rename ρ. Intersection and the joins are derived (∩ = R − (R − S);
R ⋈_θ S = σ_θ(R × S)). A language is *relationally complete* when it expresses at least what this
algebra expresses.

"Expresses" is made exact by fixing an encoding and a decoding: a term `T` expresses an algebra
expression `E` when `dec(eval(T, enc(db))) = E(db)` for every database instance `db`.

## 2. The encoding: a relation is a delta set, a tuple is a delta

**Definition 1 (enc).** Fix an encoding author `α` and timestamp `τ` (constants of the encoding).
A tuple `t = (v₁ … vₙ)` of relation `R(A₁ … Aₙ)` encodes as one delta whose claims carry a
*filing pointer* `{role: "rel", target: {id: "rel:R", context: "tuples"}}` plus one pointer
`{role: Aᵢ, target: vᵢ}` per attribute, each `vᵢ` a primitive. An instance `r` encodes as
`enc(r) = { tupleDelta(t) : t ∈ r }`. The *relation extent* is the term
`R̂ ≔ select(hasPointer({targetEntity: "rel:R"}), input)`.

**Definition 2 (dec).** Reading back is the standing lens pipeline, nothing bespoke:
`group(byTargetContext)` at root `rel:R` files every tuple delta under `tuples`, and SPEC-5 §2.1
candidate extraction — filing pointer excluded, several non-filing pointers rendered as an object
keyed by role — reconstructs exactly the record `{A₁: v₁, …, Aₙ: vₙ}`.

**Lemma 2 (identity alignment).** Under Definition 1, delta id is injective on tuples: with
author and timestamp fixed, claims vary only with the attribute pointers, and canonical CBOR is
injective on claims (SPEC-1 §4.1). Hence the algebra's id-keyed set operations coincide with
Codd's tuple-keyed ones, and duplicate insertion is a no-op — **set semantics is not enforced by
any operator; it is content addressing**. ∎

Union-compatibility note: Codd's ∪/−/∩ are defined over union-compatible operands. In this
encoding that means same-marker extents — sub-instances of one relation symbol, as the vectors
exercise. Operands under *distinct* markers are re-keyed by a derivation (a rename-flavored
re-assertion, Theorem 3).

Provenance caveat, which is a feature in caveat's clothing: over deltas of *heterogeneous*
provenance, id-difference is not value-difference — the same claim by two authors is two deltas.
The encoding pins `(α, τ)` precisely so Codd's semantics falls out; lift the pin and you get
something Codd cannot say at all — *who asserts each row*, queryable in σ (§7).

## 3. The structural fact everything turns on

**Lemma 1 (no minting).** For every DSet-sort term `T` and input set `I`: `eval(T, I) ⊆ I`. No
operator constructs a delta.

*Proof.* Structural induction over the §9 grammar. `input` evaluates to `I`; `select` ⊆ its
operand (§4.1); `union(L,R)` ⊆ eval(L) ∪ eval(R); `intersect`/`difference` return members of
their first operand (§4.9); `mask` under every policy returns a subset of its operand (§4.3 —
`annotate` adds a tag channel, not members); the `inView` sub-evaluation (§3.1) is itself a
DSet-sort term over the ambient input and only narrows the enclosing predicate. The HView-sort
operators (`group`, `expand`, `prune`) restructure entries drawn from their operands, and
`resolve` maps those entries' candidates to values — none introduces a delta not in `I`. ∎

**Corollary 1 (complexity envelope).** |eval(T, I)| ≤ |I| for DSet results — the reason the
normative O(|D|·|t|) bound (§5) is satisfiable at all. A product-shaped operator has output
Ω(|D|²) and is excluded by the envelope before it is excluded by the grammar.

**Corollary 2 (sandboxing by construction).** A federated peer's term can select from, reshape,
and resolve what you already hold — nothing else. No received query can amplify, mint, or cost
more than a linear scan (§1, "safe federation"). In SQL, a hostile federated query can
cross-product you into the ground; here that attack is grammatically unwritable.

## 4. Theorem 1 — the selective fragment, constructively

**Theorem 1.** Over Definition 1's encoding, every expression built from attribute-vs-constant
selection (with ∧ ∨ ¬ and the extended comparators), union, difference, intersection, and
projection-with-lineage is expressed by an L2 term, exactly (`dec ∘ eval ∘ enc = E`).

| Codd | L2 term | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| σ_{A θ c} | `select(hasPointer({role: {exact: "A"}, targetValue: {vcmp: {cmp: θ, value: c}}}), R̂)` | θ ∈ {eq, neq, lt, lte, gt, gte, prefix, inSet} plus `between` — strictly more than Codd's θ-set. Every encoded tuple carries every attribute, so `not(hasPointer(…))` and the negated comparator coincide. |
| ∪ | `union(R̂₁, R̂₂)` | Id-keyed; by Lemma 2, exactly tuple union. |
| − | `difference(of, without)` | §4.9; by Lemma 2, exactly Codd's −. |
| ∩ | `intersect(left, right)` | §4.9; equals R − (R − S), pinned as a vector. |
| π_A (with lineage) | `prune({inSet: [A…, "rel"]}, group(byTargetContext, R̂))` | Entries stay keyed by source-tuple id — projection *with lineage*. |

Proof is by the constructions plus Lemma 2; the positive half of
`vectors/l1-eval/eval-relational.json` pins each row byte-exactly. ∎

**Projection's duplicates.** Codd's π eliminates duplicates; the HView keeps them, distinguished
by lineage. Three collapses exist: (i) at the View boundary — Views are canonical CBOR, so
value-keyed dedup is a trivial deterministic host step; (ii) `all(order, distinct: true)` —
designed here and **built the same day** (issue #33, ERRATA-5 R9, pinned by
`vectors/l1-eval/eval-distinct.json`): an additive, parse-visible schema extension (no `alg`
bump, §8) that orders candidates and keeps the first occurrence of each distinct value, equality
being the View's canonical bytes; (iii) by re-assertion — a derived author emitting each
projected record as a fresh tuple delta gets duplicate elimination *for free*, because identical
projections have identical claims and therefore the same content address. `DISTINCT` as an
operator is (ii); `DISTINCT` as a fact about the substrate is (iii) — identity.

**Selection between two attributes.** σ_{A=B} — comparing two values of the same tuple — is not
expressible: a `PPred`'s fields must hold on a single pointer (§3), and cross-pointer comparison
is the arbitrary predicate the algebra excludes. Same gap as product, same two routes (§5). SQL
engines evaluate θ-selections by iterating too; they hide the iteration inside the executor.
Rhizomatic declines to hide it.

## 5. Theorem 2 — the gap, proven and then defended

**Theorem 2 (product is inexpressible in L2).** No L2 term computes R × S under any encoding in
which distinct tuples encode as distinct deltas.

*Proof.* |R × S| = |R|·|S| pair-records, present in neither enc(R) nor enc(S). By Lemma 1 every
DSet result is a subset of the input, and every HView/View entry derives from input deltas'
claims — no result can contain a record no input delta carries. For |R|, |S| ≥ 2 even the
cardinality is unreachable. ∎

So pure L2 is not Codd-complete, and the failure is definitional: ×'s job is to mint tuples,
minting is the one power the read algebra renounces (Lemma 1), and the renunciation is what the
complexity envelope and the federation sandbox are made of (Corollaries 1–2). Admitting a ninth
operator — §10's open question — would not extend the algebra; it would forfeit two normative
guarantees to buy expressiveness the system already has elsewhere. **The answer to §10 is no,
and provably not as a matter of taste.**

The deeper reading is native to this substrate: a Codd row is anonymous — nobody asserts it, it
merely results. In a provenance-carrying substrate every delta has an author, so a product tuple
entering the data is a *claim*, and claims need someone to make them. The two sanctioned routes
are the two answers to "who asserts the pair?":

- **Route 1 — the author, at write time.** A delta is an n-ary role-labeled hyperedge:
  `{buyer, seller, item, price}` in one delta *is* a pre-joined row across four entity "tables."
  Rhizomatic stores materialized joins as its native shape; `expand` is join *navigation*
  (declarative, DAG-terminating), never join computation. Most joins applications actually run
  are of this kind.
- **Route 2 — a derived author, at read-become-write time.** Ad-hoc product/θ-join is a pure
  function of two views, so it is a derivation (SPEC-7): an identified function emitting pair
  records as signed deltas, `rhizomatic.derived.from` pinning the exact input hashes. The join
  acquires what SQL's executor never gives it: an identity, a cache that is just data
  (content-addressed, reactor-maintained), an audit trail (`explain`), and third-party replay
  verification. The query plan becomes a citizen.

**Theorem 3 (stratified completeness of the system).** For every relational-algebra expression E
there is a finite stratified pipeline — each stratum an L2 term or an L7 derivation whose output
re-enters as deltas — with `dec(eval(pipeline, enc(db))) = E(db)`, deterministically.

*Proof sketch.* Induction on E. Leaves are relation extents. σ_const, ∪, −, ∩, π-with-lineage
compose within a stratum (Theorem 1). Each ×, θ-join, σ_{A=B}, or set-π node becomes one
derivation stratum: a pure function of its operand views, emitting result tuples under
Definition 1 (fresh marker, the derived author's identity, fixed timestamp) — deterministic,
idempotent (content addressing dedups re-runs), convergent (union CRDT). Rename ρ needs no
stratum: by standard normalization renames commute to the root — View-layer prop naming
(SPEC-5) — or into product inputs, where the emitting derivation chooses the roles. Stratum
count ≤ |E|. Lag is the honest price: one derivation cascade per stratum (SPEC-7 §5) — the same
price §3.1 names for recursive trusted sets. ∎

## 7. What "fully relational but SQL-free" means

Codd's thesis was never tables and never SQL; it was data independence, sets over
pointer-chasing, and an algebra machines can rewrite. Rhizomatic keeps the commitments and sheds
the accretion:

- **Relations are lenses, not storage.** The substrate stores authored hyperedges; any relation
  is a group/prune/resolve view, and two applications may hold different relational readings of
  one substrate simultaneously. Data independence past Codd: independence from any privileged
  logical schema at all.
- **No NULL.** L1 cannot represent null (SPEC-1 §2.1); per-lens defaulting is `absentAs`,
  visible in the schema. Every predicate is two-valued; the three-valued-logic tax SQL pays on
  every WHERE clause is never levied. This proof never had to mention ⊥ — itself a result.
- **Superposition instead of UPDATE.** Contradictory claims coexist; `resolve` collapses per
  declared policy, per reader; negation is first-class, auditable, revocable. Time-travel is
  `σ_{timestamp ≤ T}` — an ordinary predicate on an ordinary column, not a plugin.
- **Provenance is just more columns.** Trust policy (`byAuthorRank`, `mask(trust)`, `inView`)
  lives in the same algebra as data queries. SQL has no column for *who says so*.
- **The optimizer inheritance survives.** Terms are inspectable data with algebraic laws and a
  decidable-subsumption goal; the reactor is incremental view maintenance over them. SQL-free is
  not optimizer-free.
- **The incompleteness is the security model.** The one primitive refused is the one whose
  output exceeds its input; the refusal *is* the sandbox and the envelope. SQL grants every
  reader the power to fabricate unbounded intermediate state; Rhizomatic relocates that power to
  L7, where it runs with an identity, by consent, and signs its work.
- **No DDL/DML split.** Schemas are terms, terms are deltas (§7 of SPEC-2): queryable, forkable,
  negatable, federate-able. The self-describing database, kept honestly.
